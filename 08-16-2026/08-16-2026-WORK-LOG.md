# August 16, 2026 work log

Today I pulled my quantum and mass-spectrometry notes into one clearer
research direction. I am starting with candidate-spectrum retrieval instead
of trying to predict a completely new molecular structure. That gives me a
smaller problem where I can test the representation and compare it with
ordinary methods.

The starting point is an observed MS/MS spectrum with fragment masses and
intensities. I want to account for normal measurement uncertainty, so I am not
treating every mass as perfectly exact. I also looked at a log-mass coordinate
and a continuous wavelet transform. The purpose of the wavelet step is to keep
information from several scales and then turn it into a fixed feature vector.
This part is classical preprocessing.

I also placed the idea next to related classical approaches: wavelet-based
features, dynamic wavelet scales, soft peak and neutral-loss kernels, and
sparse peak interactions. Those methods give me the comparisons I need before
making any claim about a quantum layer.

The quantum part only starts after those features are made. An independent
qubit encoding is easy to write down, but its similarity kernel has a classical
equivalent. Because of that, the more interesting direction is to represent
relationships between features. The possible relationships include fragment
coactivation, neutral losses, nearby wavelet scales, and local mass patterns.
I described these relationships with a sparse graph and used spectrum-
dependent interactions as a possible quantum or quantum-inspired layer.

I kept the chemistry requirements visible throughout the notes. Precursor
neutral mass, charge, adduct, ionization mode, isotope compatibility, fragment
agreement, and neutral-loss evidence should constrain or score the candidates.
The quantum similarity value should not replace those checks.

The earlier Grover notebook helped me recognize the search pattern, but I am
not claiming that Grover is the final method. The real question is how to
define a useful candidate-evaluation rule from the chemistry. Depending on
the final problem, a quantum walk, QAOA, Bayesian model, graph method, or
dynamic programming may be more appropriate.

I also noted a possible future connection to DIA and retention-time or wave
alignment. DIA creates an assignment problem because several precursors can
contribute to one acquisition window. Alignment confidence could eventually
help define relationships between signals, but I need the actual alignment
formulation before adding it to the model.
